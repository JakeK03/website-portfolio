From Portland, OR to New York City, Jake Kuerbis is a designer currently attending Parsons School of Design.

Currently exploring the influence of human activity on natural spaces and the implementation of food to create visual identities through design. Reach out on insta or email.

Ghost Towns.2025:

Inspired by 19th and early 20th century frontier mining towns in Oregon. This book looks into the decay of the final American frontier. What once was, and now is. The ultimate journey of prosperity and finally destruction. What is left is a hidden valley of tall grass, junkyards, old railway’s, and doomed mining ghost-towns. How can looking at the past, inform the future on human interaction with the natural landscape?

Impossible Produce.2025:

Impossible Produce sets out to explore the calamities of American global food production, and imports of food products to survive the growing market needs of consumers. How has tariffs affected the impossibility of providing fresh fruits and vegetables year round?

Pa–Per.2024:

A Co-collaboration in Barcelona, set on utilizing bread scraps. Barcelona’s food culture was an inspiration for the food design project. Bread scraps are a big part of food waste, and the goal was set out to implement it as an organic material in a way to close the loop on recycling sustainability. The paper was made entirely out of bread scraps and paper bread bags sourced from a local bakery. This was created in part with Vincenzo Frontino and Issey Huang.

Cannes Festival Design.2024:

Inspired by camera shutters and viewports, this motion graphic course project was a redesign of a current brand or company.
